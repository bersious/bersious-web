01 · The idea

Learning trade-offs by making them

Space mission design is usually taught as separate subjects — orbital mechanics here, spacecraft structures there, launch vehicles somewhere else. What students rarely get is the whole system at once, where adding a better instrument quietly forces a heavier spacecraft, a bigger rocket, and a downlink that cannot keep up.

That gap is the game. You are not told the right answer, because there usually isn't one. You are told the consequences.

02 · How a run works

Five screens, one mission

  • Briefing — pick a scenario: a climate record, a disaster-response satellite, a space-weather sentinel.
  • Design — bus, power source, instruments, orbit, launch vehicle. Resource bars respond live.
  • Compatibility gates — mass with margin vs. launcher capacity, power draw vs. generation, data vs. downlink.
  • Simulation — phased mission with seeded random events, so the same design can be replayed and shared.
  • Debrief — a score, plus an itemised list of why it failed and what would have fixed it.

That last screen is the point of the whole thing. A score tells you that you lost. An explanation tells you what you misunderstood.
